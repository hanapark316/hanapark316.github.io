Job Market Paper

When Relatability Backfires in Human and Virtual Influencer Self-Presentation

Hana Park and Hyunmin Lee

Abstract
This study examines how audiences respond to human and virtual influencers' self-presentation strategies in influencer-mediated strategic communication. Drawing on Expectancy Violations Theory, the study proposes that relatable and aspirational self-presentation may be evaluated differently depending on whether the communicator is a human influencer or a virtual influencer. Using a 2 × 2 between-subjects experiment, the study will test the effects of influencer type and self-presentation strategy on perceived expectancy violation valence, attitude toward the organization, social media engagement intention, and campaign supportive intention. By comparing how the same self-presentation cues are interpreted across human and virtual communicators, this study contributes to research on influencer communication, virtual influencers, and expectancy violations in digital public relations contexts.

Publications

Performing Communicative Legitimacy: English-Language Responses to Spanish Posts in Multilingual News Spaces

Hana Park and Asta Zelenkauskaite

Studies in Communication and Media, 2026

Abstract
This study examines how language is used to negotiate communicative hierarchies online. Using a mirroring design, we compare English- and Spanish-language user responses to Spanish-language posts by The New York Times on X to assess how audiences interpret, contest, or delegitimize linguistic diversity within U.S.-based digital news media spaces. Findings show that English-language comments appeared more frequently than Spanish-language comments on Spanish-language posts, and that unrelated types of comments were substantially more common in English than in Spanish. Whereas English-language unrelated comments frequently targeted The New York Times for its use of Spanish and framed multilingualism as an illegitimate intrusion, Spanish-language unrelated comments were less frequent and were dominated primarily by political campaigning rather than language policing.

Framing Generative AI: An Analysis of Impact, Quoted Sources, and Attributions of Responsibilities in U.S. Elite Media

Hyunmin Lee and Hana Park

Journalism & Mass Communication Quarterly, 2026

Abstract
This paper examined how elite news media frame the impact of generative AI on the cultural, social, economic, and political domains of life and analyzed the different types of ethical concerns surrounding generative AI and to whom the media attribute responsibility for creating and solving those concerns. The results of a content analysis (N = 2,879) of The New York Times and The Wall Street Journal show that societal and economic impacts were the most frequently discussed when reporting about generative AI.
